What's Included in a PJL Spring Opening — Bolton

Every spring opening in Bolton includes a full 10-point system check before we leave your property:

  • ✓ Slow-pressurize the main supply line to prevent water-hammer damage to PVC fittings
  • ✓ Test each zone individually for correct pressure, coverage, and rotation
  • ✓ Check every sprinkler head for winter damage, misalignment, or clogged nozzles
  • ✓ Adjust arc and throw on rotors and pop-up sprays for full coverage
  • ✓ Inspect the manifold and valve box for cracks, leaks, and solenoid faults
  • ✓ Verify the Hydrawise (or other smart controller) Wi-Fi connection and program
  • ✓ Set the seasonal watering schedule against the GTA's clay-loam soil profile
  • ✓ Confirm rain-sensor function (where installed) — required for water-bylaw compliance
  • ✓ Flag any backflow assembly issues for referral to a certified Ontario backflow tester
  • ✓ Walk you through the system and answer any questions on the spot

Backflow testing is a separately-licensed trade in Ontario. PJL does not service backflow assemblies but can refer you to a certified tester if yours needs annual recertification.

Bolton Sprinkler FAQs

When should I open my sprinkler system in Bolton?

In Bolton, the safe window for spring opening is typically the second week of April through mid-May, after the last hard frost. Opening too early risks freeze damage to backflow assemblies and exposed risers. We run the Bolton/Caledon corridor weekly during peak season — book a week or two ahead to ride the regular route.

What does sprinkler repair cost in Bolton?

Our service call (mobilization + on-site assessment) is $95. Repair labour is billed at $95 from there. Common parts costs: sprinkler head replacement $68, pipe break repair from $120, full 3-valve manifold rebuild starting at $135 plus valves. If our AI-Smart-Intake diagnostic tool correctly predicts the fix, you get one hour of repair labour free on the diagnosed work — our only standing discount.

Do you offer same-day sprinkler repair in Bolton?

Bolton sits on a regular weekly Caledon route. Same-day is possible for active leaks but isn't the default — we typically batch Bolton repairs onto the regular weekly Caledon route. Book 5-7 days ahead for non-urgent repairs to ride a scheduled visit.

Are there watering bylaws in Bolton I need to know about?

Bolton sits in the Town of Caledon within the Region of Peel. Peel does not enforce a permanent mandatory odd/even watering schedule — instead the Region operates a multi-stage framework that activates only when supply is constrained. The standing ask from the Region is to skip Wednesdays for outdoor watering. We program controllers to a sensible default (early-morning watering, 3 days per week) and can switch to stricter compliance if Peel escalates to a mandatory stage.

How deep do sprinkler lines need to be buried in Ontario?

The Ontario frost line is approximately 1.2 metres (4 feet), but irrigation lines don't need to be buried that deep — they're drained and pressurized only during the warm months. PJL buries mainlines 8–12 inches deep and laterals 6–8 inches deep, which is standard for southern Ontario. Fall winterization (compressor blowout) is what protects them from freeze damage, not depth.

Can you upgrade my existing system to a Hydrawise smart controller?

Yes — Hydrawise retrofits are one of our specialties. Patrick is Hunter-certified through Hunter University, and we install Hunter HPC-400 Hydrawise WiFi controllers on existing systems regularly. Pricing: $595 for 1–4 zones, $750 for 5–7 zones, $1,195 for 8–16 zones, all-in. Typical install takes under 90 minutes.
