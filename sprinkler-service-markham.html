What's Included in a PJL Spring Opening — Markham

Every spring opening in Markham includes a full 10-point system check before we leave your property:

  • ✓ Slow-pressurize the main supply line to prevent water-hammer damage to PVC fittings
  • ✓ Test each zone individually for correct pressure, coverage, and rotation
  • ✓ Check every sprinkler head for winter damage, misalignment, or clogged nozzles
  • ✓ Adjust arc and throw on rotors and pop-up sprays for full coverage
  • ✓ Inspect the manifold and valve box for cracks, leaks, and solenoid faults
  • ✓ Verify the Hydrawise (or other smart controller) Wi-Fi connection and program
  • ✓ Set the seasonal watering schedule against the GTA's clay-loam soil profile
  • ✓ Confirm rain-sensor function (where installed) — required for water-bylaw compliance
  • ✓ Flag any backflow assembly issues for referral to a certified Ontario backflow tester
  • ✓ Walk you through the system and answer any questions on the spot

Backflow testing is a separately-licensed trade in Ontario. PJL does not service backflow assemblies but can refer you to a certified tester if yours needs annual recertification.

Markham Sprinkler FAQs

When should I open my sprinkler system in Markham?

In Markham, the safe window for spring opening is typically the second week of April through mid-May, after the last hard frost. Opening too early risks freeze damage to backflow assemblies and exposed risers. We start booking in late March and prioritize repeat customers first — book early to lock in your preferred week.

What does sprinkler repair cost in Markham?

Our service call (mobilization + on-site assessment) is $95. Repair labour is billed at $95 from there. Are there watering bylaws in Markham I need to know about?

Markham falls under York Region's Outdoor Water-Use Bylaw with stricter local variations. From June 1 to September 30, residential watering is permitted on odd or even calendar days matching your house number, in EITHER the 6 a.m. to 9 a.m. window OR the 6 p.m. to 9 p.m. window — not both. New sod and seed get a two-month grace period. Commercial properties are limited to a three-hour window between midnight and 6 a.m. We program your controller to comply during spring opening.

How deep do sprinkler lines need to be buried in Ontario?

The Ontario frost line in York Region is approximately 1.2 metres (4 feet), but irrigation lines don't need to be buried that deep — they're drained and pressurized only during the warm months. PJL buries mainlines 8–12 inches deep and laterals 6–8 inches deep, which is standard for the GTA. Fall winterization (compressor blowout) is what protects them from freeze damage, not depth.
