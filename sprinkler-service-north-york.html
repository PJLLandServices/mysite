The North York lots PJL works most.

North York covers a lot of ground — roughly Steeles to the 401, Bathurst to Victoria Park — and the irrigation challenge changes block by block. A 6,000 ft² Don Mills bungalow on a flat 1950s lot doesn't need the same system as a 0.8-acre Bridle Path estate with a ravine drop at the back. We treat each as its own design problem.

Estate multi-zone systems

York Mills, Hoggs Hollow, Bridle Path, St Andrew-Windfields. Properties that need 10 to 20 zones to manage front, side and rear lawn areas, hedge drip overlays, perimeter rotors, and often separate zones for the front-curb hedge and the back-garden lawn. Hunter HPC-400 Hydrawise controller with PCM modules; pressure-regulated heads on the long throws.

Ravine & sloped lots

York Mills, Hoggs Hollow, parts of Bayview Woods and Don Mills near the East Don. The West Don, East Don and Wilket Creek ravine systems cut through North York, so any property that backs onto a ravine has slope to manage. Pressure-regulated heads, matched-precipitation nozzles, hydrozoning by slope direction — runoff is the enemy and the bylaw notice that follows it.

Teardown / rebuild new installs

Willowdale, Bayview Village, parts of Bayview Woods. The rebuild belt north of the 401. Best practice is to rough-in the sprinkler mainline and sleeves before the driveway and walkways get poured, then come back after sod down to install heads and tune. We coordinate with the GC where needed and run independent of the lighting and landscape trades.

Mature canopy work

Almost every older North York street. Toronto's Private Tree By-law (Municipal Code Chapter 658) protects any private tree with a trunk diameter of 30 cm or more. Trenching through the protected root zone can require a permit. We walk the property before quoting and route around protected trees — even if it means more pipe.

What we check on a North York spring opening

The standard system check, plus a few items specific to the larger North York systems:

  • ✓ Slow re-pressurize the main supply line to protect older fittings on systems that have been in the ground 20-plus years
  • ✓ Test every zone individually — not just the first three — for pressure, coverage and rotation
  • ✓ Inspect each head for winter damage, misalignment from snow plough impact, or root-related tilt
  • ✓ Adjust arc and throw on rotors so the long throws don't overshoot onto hardscape or walkways
  • ✓ Check manifold boxes — including the back-zone manifolds that are easy to forget on estate properties
  • ✓ Verify Hydrawise Wi-Fi connection; on weak-signal lots we recommend extender placement
  • ✓ Program seasonal schedule with cycle-and-soak on any zones that sit on slope
  • ✓ Test rain sensor function (where installed) and flag any backflow assembly issues for a certified Ontario backflow tester
  • ✓ Walk you through any issues found and quote them as separate line items before any repair work begins

North York sprinkler FAQs

Which North York neighbourhoods does PJL serve?

North York is our anchor neighbourhood in Toronto. We work Willowdale, Bayview Village, York Mills, Hoggs Hollow, the Bridle Path, Don Mills and Bayview Woods most often, plus the Banbury and St Andrew-Windfields pockets in between. The common thread is properties large enough that a designed sprinkler system pays back its cost — typically 5 to 16 zones residentially, with the larger estates pushing past that.

What makes irrigation in North York different from other GTA areas?

Three things. First, lot size: estate-scale properties in Hoggs Hollow, York Mills and the Bridle Path commonly need 10 to 20 zones; that takes proper design, not a template. Second, terrain: parts of North York sit on or beside the West Don, East Don and Wilket Creek ravines — sloped lots that need pressure-regulated heads and hydrozoning to avoid runoff. Third, the mature tree canopy: trenching anywhere near a 30 cm-trunk-or-larger tree triggers Toronto's Private Tree By-law so we route mainlines accordingly.

Do you handle teardown-rebuild new installs in Willowdale?

Yes — this is one of our most frequent North York job types. Willowdale and Bayview Village have a lot of teardown-rebuild custom homes where the irrigation system goes in alongside the final landscape. The cleanest sequence is: rough-in the mainline and sleeves before the hardscape is poured, install the heads and laterals after the sod is down. We coordinate with the GC where needed.

How quickly can you get to a North York repair call?

North York sits about 50 minutes from our Newmarket base via the 404 and 401. We book Toronto repair calls on a priority basis — usually 1-3 business days during peak season (April through October). When a route is already heading down, we can sometimes consolidate next-day. We do not advertise a same-day guarantee for Toronto because honest dispatch from an hour away can't promise that.

Can you design a multi-zone system for a Bridle Path or Hoggs Hollow estate?

Yes, this is core to our installation work in North York. Estate properties commonly need 12-20 zones to manage front, side and rear lawn areas plus discreet drip overlay on hedges and beds, often with separate zones for back-garden lawn and front-curb hedges. Designs use Hunter heads, Hunter HPC-400 Hydrawise smart controller with PCM modules for the higher zone counts, pressure-regulated heads on sloped sections, and matched-precipitation nozzles so adjacent zones run for compatible cycle times.

Do you do landscape lighting alongside the irrigation?

Yes — we run a landscape lighting service that pairs well with irrigation installs on the estate lots. LED, marine-grade fixtures, app-controlled. On the larger North York properties the same crew can route lighting cable alongside irrigation laterals during a new install, which saves trenching cost.
