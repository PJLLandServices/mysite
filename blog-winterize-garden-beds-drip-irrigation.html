Getting garden beds ready for winter when they're on drip irrigation.

In this article

Fall garden cleanup is simple enough on its own. Add a drip line running through the beds and a few things change: where you can dig, when you should mulch, and what you can safely leave alone. Get it wrong and the first sign is a zone that sprays like a fountain when the system starts up in spring.

Our team doesn't do garden cleanup or mulching. We install and service the drip zones underneath. Here's how to put your beds to bed without damaging them.

Does drip irrigation need to be winterized?

Quick answer

Yes. Drip zones are cleared with compressed air along with every other zone during a fall closing. The drip line itself is flexible, but the zone valve and the filter and pressure regulator at the start of most drip zones still hold water that can freeze and crack.

You don't need to pull the drip line out of the beds, and you shouldn't. It's designed to stay in place year-round. Just make sure the drip zones are included when your fall closing is done — they're part of the system, not a separate hose setup.

What should I do before digging in beds that have drip line?

Quick answer

Find the drip line before you dig. Pull back the mulch along a few rows, or take phone photos of where the lines run. Bulb planters, spades and edging tools are the most common way drip line gets cut in the fall.

A nick in the line doesn't show up until spring start-up, when that spot sprays instead of drips. By then the bed is planted and the hole is hard to find.

A few habits that prevent it:

  • Mark the lines with small plant stakes before planting bulbs or dividing perennials.
  • Dig by hand with a trowel close to known lines, not a power auger.
  • Plant bulbs between rows, not directly on top of them.

Should I cut back perennials in the fall?

Quick answer

Cut back plants that were diseased or collapse into mush, like hostas and peonies. Leave healthy perennials and ornamental grasses standing. They trap snow for insulation, shelter beneficial insects, and make it easier to see the bed's layout — and your drip line — in spring.

Pull out annuals and spent vegetable plants. Clear out fallen fruit and diseased leaves, since those carry problems into next year.

When should I mulch garden beds for winter?

Quick answer

Add winter mulch after the ground has started to freeze, usually late November in York Region. The goal is to keep the soil frozen, not warm it. Mulching too early gives mice a warm place to nest and can delay plants going dormant.

Good news for drip owners: mulch over the drip line is fine. Quality drip line is made to sit under mulch, and mulch protects it from sun and damage. Just follow two rules:

  • Keep mulch a few centimetres away from stems and trunks.
  • Don't bury valve boxes. We need to lift those lids in spring. A valve box under 10 cm of mulch is hard to find and easy to crack with a shovel.

What about pots and containers on drip tubing?

Quick answer

Before emptying or moving a container, lift the drip tubing out of the pot and lay the end on top of the bed. Don't leave the tubing end buried in potting soil or tucked under a pot on the patio, where it can get crushed or plugged.

Bring clay and ceramic pots inside, or tip them upside down. Water in the soil freezes and cracks them.

Do I still need to water garden beds after the sprinklers are off?

Quick answer

Evergreens, new plantings and anything planted this year benefit from a deep watering during dry spells until the ground freezes. Established perennials usually get enough from fall rain. Hand-water with a hose before you shut off your outdoor taps for winter.

Cedars and other evergreens need this most. See Why Cedars Turn Brown Over Winter.

Your garden bed winter checklist

  1. Before the closing: Plant bulbs and divide perennials while the drip line is still easy to see. Mark the lines first.
  2. Fall closing: Drip zones are blown out with the rest of the system.
  3. October–November: Pull annuals and diseased plants. Leave healthy perennials standing. Deep-water evergreens in dry spells.
  4. Before freeze-up: Empty and store pots. Lift drip tubing out of containers.
  5. Late November: Mulch once the ground starts freezing. Keep valve boxes uncovered.
  6. Spring: Drip zones are checked when the system is started up.

Still watering beds with spray heads?

Spray heads soak the leaves, miss the roots, and waste water on paths. Most of the beds we see across York Region would do better on drip. Read why we're switching most GTA garden beds to drip, or see our drip irrigation service. Fall is a good time to plan a spring conversion.
