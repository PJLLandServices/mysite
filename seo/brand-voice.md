## Voice & Tone

- **"Call the problem the problem, show it, and give the fix."** That is
  the voice, in Patrick's words. Name what is wrong, show the evidence
  (the pressure reading, the wet spot, the valve that is not clicking),
  lay out the options with their costs. No wind-up, no softening.
- Extremely honest, including when honesty loses the job: if a system
  does not need replacing, say so. Repair vs replace is maths, never
  pressure.
- Plain words, contractions, short paragraphs. A neighbour who happens to
  do irrigation for a living. "Yeah, that's annoying" is allowed; "we
  strive to provide" is not.
- Practical. Every piece solves one real homeowner problem and ends with
  a clear next step.
- Acknowledge the frustration in one sentence, then get to the fix. The
  people reading this have usually already been let down once (see
  Positioning), so do not oversell — just be reachable and clear.
- No hype, no fake urgency, no exclamation marks. One emoji at most,
  usually none.

## Positioning

**What PJL sells, and to whom.** Repair, maintenance and takeover of
existing sprinkler systems, plus design and installation, across York
Region and the GTA from a Newmarket base. Three customer groups:

1. **Homeowners** with an existing system — typical residential through
   estate lots, with most interest coming from mid-to-high-value
   properties.
2. **Landscape installers and designers** who need an irrigation partner
   on their projects.
3. **Commercial property owners and managers.**

**Why they call.** The number-one inbound call is a customer who cannot
reach their current contractor any more. Scheduling slipped, messages
went unanswered, and they went looking for someone new. Close behind:
a system inherited with the house that nobody explained. Content should
speak to that person — someone already let down — not to a first-time
buyer.

**The pain PJL solves.** Two things: being reachable and available, and
diagnosing correctly the first time. The second comes from a knowledge
base wider than irrigation alone — hydraulics and plumbing
infrastructure, mechanical repair, project management, reading a site,
and what turf actually needs to establish.

**What PJL says that competitors do not:**

- **Install-only companies build it and move on.** Their service side
  does not go deep enough for customers who need the extra attention.
  PJL is the service-depth company: it will take over a system someone
  else installed, explain what it can and cannot do, and stay reachable.
- **Their optional upgrades are PJL's standard.** On every new install:
  professional Hunter Hydrawise controllers with built-in Wi-Fi,
  expandable with plug-in modules as zones are added; pressure-regulated
  sprinkler heads on swing-arm connections for head stability; efficient
  nozzles that cut water per head, which allows larger zones and shorter
  run times per cycle; and professionally managed over-the-air controller
  updates and management, included.
- **Prices are published** — on the website, on the quote, on the
  invoice — and the quote number is the invoice number.
- **Three-year warranty on parts and labour** on installs. **Same-day
  repair, April–October**, and if the day cannot be reached the customer
  is told on the call rather than left waiting.
- **Hunter and Rain Bird parts only.** Free 45-minute on-site design
  consultation.
- Relationship-driven, not transactional: no seasonal upsells pushed into
  a repair conversation; the customer portal is where seasonal booking
  lives, on the customer's own time.

## Core Topics (genuine expertise — comfortable being challenged on)

- **Hydraulics and plumbing design infrastructure.** The water supply is
  the component a sprinkler system is built on: supply, pressure, flow,
  backflow protection, and matching the system to the source.
- **Water distribution and load correlation** — zone loading, head
  spacing and nozzle selection against available flow, run times against
  what the turf needs.
- **Controller management** — Hunter Hydrawise setup, Wi-Fi changes,
  offline recovery, new-homeowner takeover, OTA management; programming
  Hunter Pro-C / X-Core / SRC, Rain Bird ESP-TM2, Orbit timers; scheduling
  that reflects actual water requirements rather than defaults.
- **Hunter Industries product lines**, in depth.
- Diagnosing faults: zones not running, heads not spraying, dry spots,
  pooling, wiring and valve problems, finding a broken pipe, leak
  detection.
- Seasonal service in a freeze-thaw climate: proper blow-outs, opening
  and closing dates in Ontario, freeze-damage costs.
- Turf establishment requirements from the watering side: new sod,
  overseeding, establishing a green lawn.
- Water use and cost: York Region and municipal watering bylaws, flow
  sensors, what smart controllers actually save.

**Not claimed:** PJL is not a horticulturist. Dense garden maintenance and
plant identification are outside the expertise (Patrick is still
learning plant ID). Drip and garden irrigation are written from the
water-delivery side only; the site does not give plant-care or
garden-design advice.

## Customer Language (the questions asked over and over)

- "How do I get a green lawn?" / "How do I establish a new lawn?"
- "How often should I water?" "How much water does it need?"
- "What does the water cost here?" (→ `water-cost-calculator.html`)
- "How do I program it?" "Does it do it automatically?"
- "How do I know if I have a leak?"
- "When do I turn the sprinkler system off?" (and on)
- "I can't get hold of my sprinkler guy." "The previous owner left this
  system and I have no idea what it does."
- "One zone isn't coming on." "A head is stuck." "There's a wet spot
  that never dries." "The controller says offline."
- They ask for a price before they ask for a visit, and compare against
  DIY and the neighbour's guy.

Most of these questions already have a page. The gap finder's first
instinct for them should be "make the existing page findable" (title,
internal links, snippet), not "write a new one".

## Things We Never Say

- Invented experience or frequency claims ("nine times out of ten", "in
  my 20 years"). The one frequency claim allowed is Patrick's own: the
  most common call is a customer who cannot reach their contractor.
- Absolute technical claims in copy. Say "expandable with add-on
  modules" and give the real zone limit for the controller model, not
  "unlimited".
- A price range or a rounded price. Prices come from `pricing.json`
  through `data-price` tokens; anything not in the price list is "a
  custom quote after a site visit".
- "Call a sprinkler company" — PJL is the sprinkler company.
- Horticultural advice: plant selection, garden care, plant diagnosis.
- Corporate filler: "I understand your concern", "we strive to",
  "industry-leading", "best in class".
- Pressure: "book now before it's too late", countdown language.
- Running down a named competitor. The contrast is "install-only vs
  service-depth", never a company name.
- Generic Ontario-wide claims where a specific town, soil, bylaw, or
  pressure fact would do.
